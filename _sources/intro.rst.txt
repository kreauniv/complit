Introduction
============

Welcome to "Programming Literacy".

What do we mean by "literacy" here?
-----------------------------------

In the simplest sense, the ability to read and write programs would need to be
included. However, programs are also unlike common languages in that we can use
a computer to **interrogate** them. This process can help us gain a richer
understanding of a given program, no matter how large or complex, help us think
about a particular domain we're working on in different ways with clarity, and,
given the nature of our times, work better with LLM generated programs in the
future.

In addition, we'll also explore some "literature" to gain a view of some common
ways of thinking that are useful when working with programs and domains.

Setup
-----

We'll be using Racket for our learning. So download and install DrRacket_ to
get going with our work. You can install and play with it on your own. We'll
also go over the environment in class.

The purpose of this course is not to teach you Racket, though it is a
"batteries included" language and environment you can use to build all kinds of
programs for yourself and commercially. We're doing this in Racket because it
is the easiest and most consistent language around that we can get started with
on our journey. It's a language that one might call "a language with a low
threshold and a high ceiling". By that, we mean it is easy to get started with
Racket compared to other languages but the language can scale to big programs
incorporating a wide variety of concepts supporting a large number of domains.

Reference
---------

A key reference for this course will be the time tested intro textbook `How to
Design Programs`_ (or **HtDP** for short).

General notes and guidelines
----------------------------

- You'll be reading and writing programs as part of this course. For this course,
  it is important that you actually type out the programs on your own and not
  copy-paste it from a source. Typing will let you engage with the program and
  gain a kind of familiarity and ease that you will not get with copy-pasta.
  
  .. warning:: Ignoring this instruction will be **your** loss.

- Do not use Q&A resources such as StackOverflow when you're in the learning
  phase. When you're already proficient and need minor clarifications now and
  then, these can be good to use since you'll have developed a sufficiently
  discriminative faculty by then. Not at this stage.

- Get used to reading documentation with intent. We'll have more to say
  about this in the course notes, but I want to point it out here to highlight
  its importance. This is not only to learn what is in the documentation,
  but also to give you a "customer side" feel so you can make your own
  choices to help readers of documentation you will be writing.

- **DO NOT** use any "generative AI" tools for this course. We're going to be
  doing very basic things that should not tax your brain or need assistance
  from tools beyond the grey matter you already possess. Again, the loss
  will be yours if you use these tools at this stage of learning.

- Whenever you face any difficulties, ask and have it resolved at the earliest
  opportunity -- this can be right there in the moment during class, later via
  email that day, or at least within the week. **DO NOT** postpone resolving
  your challenges with me beyond a week.

  Since July 2026 is the first term we're offering this course, there will be
  some uncertainties about how our plan for you works. Keep the communication
  going so we can make progress and improve the material and course sooner
  rather than later.

- You will not need any preparation time for your exams and quizzes if you're
  paying attention and doing assigned work regularly. A good phrase to keep
  in mind is "a sense of urgency" (borrowed from the chef whose kitchen design
  was used in "Ratatouille"). Don't rush it, but spend the time you need to
  in a relaxed yet steady manner.

- Assignment solutions will be posted soon after the deadline. This means
  submission deadlines are not negotiable.
