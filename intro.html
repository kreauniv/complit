Introduction¶

Welcome to “Programming Literacy”.

What do we mean by “literacy” here?¶

In the simplest sense, the ability to read and write programs would need to be included. However, programs are also unlike common languages in that we can use a computer to interrogate them. This process can help us gain better understanding of a given program, no matter how large or complex, help us think about a particular domain we’re working on in different ways with clarity, and, given the nature of times, work better with LLM generated programs.

In addition, we’ll also explore some “literature” to gain a view of some common ways of thinking that are useful when working with programs and domains.

Setup¶

We’ll be using Racket for our learning. So download and install DrRacket to get going with our work. You can install and play with it on your own. We’ll also go over the environment in class.

The purpose of this course is not to teach you Racket, though it is a “batteries included” language and environment you can use to build all kinds of programs for yourself and commercially. We’re doing this in Racket because it is the easiest and most consistent language around that we can get started with on our journey. It’s a language that one might call “a language with a low threshold and a high ceiling”. By that, we mean it is easy to get started with Racket compared to other languages but the language can scale to big programs incorporating a wide variety of concepts supporting a large number of domains.

Reference¶

A key reference for this course will be the time tested intro textbook How to Design Programs (or HtDP for short).
